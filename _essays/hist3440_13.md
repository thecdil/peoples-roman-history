## The Roman Empire and Imperial Cult in 2nd Century C.E.

The imperial cult acted as a foundational medium for Roman Emperors to ensure loyalty and support for themselves and the Empire itself. It had a presence all over the Empire, from Syria to England. The cult used temples that turned Emperors into gods and they were what grounded the cult within cities and provinces. Within these temples, sacrifices, prayers and political gatherings occurred, binding religion with politics. Elites and politicians alike utilized these spaces, making them an extremely important setting for understanding power dynamics and Roman politics.

In the later years of the 2nd Century C.E. The Roman Empire had many major political changes with the reign of Diocletian. The novel division of the Roman Empire into East and West, to be reined by Diocletian and Maximum, along with two Caesars each was one of the most prominent alterations to the Empire. With the division into two different areas, Nicomedia, a city of over 100,000 residents, would be deemed the capital of the Eastern Empire and home of Diocletian. Due to this, the imperial cult was destined to gain a much stronger influence within the city through the development of new temples within the imperial cult complex.

## A Look Into the Daily Life of an Imperial Priest in late 2nd Century Nicomedia

Gaius Julius Apollinaris is a 47 year old imperial Cult priest, born in Nicomedia(Modern Izmut, Turkey) in 246 C.E. He is a married man with one adult child being groomed for civic life. In his daily life he treats ritual as politics. Creating plans, processions, and making funding choices are how he makes alliances and connections. He knows exactly who notices, who resents, and who benefits within communities of elites. He is considered an elite himself and has strong ties with extremely powerful people within the Empire and Nicomedia itself.

To start out the day, Gaius would have woken up with the sunrise to perform sacrifices and offerings. We can imagine Gaius Apollinaris would wake up early and dress in his formal toga. Being that he is a political elite and relatively wealthy, he has a comfortable private home within the city of Nicomedia. He also has a fair number of slaves that assist him with dressing and preparing for his religious duties. Following his initial wake up and preparation for the day, he would travel by a litter, which would be carried by his slaves, to his respective city temple. Today, he will be arriving at the newly built imperial cult complex of Nicomedia. This temple had just been built at Emperor Diocletian's prompting, to help signify Nicomedia’s significance as the Eastern Roman Empire’s capital. The city has seen a great amount of change as it has become an administrative hub of the region.

After arriving at the cult complex, Giuas will perform his daily ritual of opening the doors to the temple dedicated to Julius Caesar and will burn the morning incense. He then lights the altars to signify the waking of Caesar. Shortly after the morning rituals have been executed, Gaius will walk just outside the temple to perform the morning augury. This would entail watching the skies to see patterns of weather or birds as they would exemplify differing omens from the gods. This would wrap up as the day grew later into the afternoon.

Soon after performing his morning rituals, Gaius would begin to meet and discuss with other social elites, such as politicians and wealthy acquaintances. This would be done to ensure connections were being made and kept and that certain political matters were discussed.

Specifically, today he is meeting with beneficiaries who will be funding another new expansion to an already impressive cult complex. Being a priest, he would also have to plan and host religious and social events. This would require a great amount of planning with other elites. We may imagine that Gaius is also preparing for a specific holiday a week from now. This day will require the sacrifice of specific animals that he is dependent on a wealthy friend and colleague to donate.

Later, Gauis must participate in even more sacrifices and rituals as he is constantly working to appease the gods. Another major duty of his is maintaining the complex, so today he will be overseeing the cleaning of statues around the temple venerating Julius Caesar. Though slaves will be performing the cleanings, he must oversee the job. He also needs to check in on the management of the courtyard that also holds statues but also a modest garden of aesthetic plants. Before long, evening comes around and Gauis is once again busy with rituals and sacrifices. Some of his duties include the lighting of more incense and the hosting of an evening service to worship a few gods. He would also perform a few prayers before overseeing the closing of the temple for the evening. He would then travel back to his private home just as he had earlier in the day, where he would prepare for a large dinner party. As the city has been growing with its new dedication as the capital of the Eastern part of the Empire, Gaius has had to host many new elites and politicians who have been moving into the area. Dinner parties such as these are an essential part of his job and social life.

## Why the Cult and Priests like Giuas are Significant

The imperial cult was one of the most useful and powerful forces in creating community to ensure loyalty to the Empire. We know that wealthy elites, just like Giuas, utilized the cult to gain popularity and relationships with other elites and the Emperor. The cult allows us to see how religion can be used as politics. By understanding the cult, we are not only able to see how power dynamics worked but also how spirituality and religion was viewed and utilized. It is crucial to see how the cult worked and achieved its purpose so that we understand how the empire lasted so long and became so large.

## A Visual Representation of the Imperial Cult

{% include essay/image.html objectid="Durham02" caption="Figure 2: The Embracing of Emperors, A Relief Excavated From the Imperial Cult Complex in Nicomedia" %}

This is one of many reliefs that were excavated in 2009 from the imperial cult complex in Nicomedia and exemplifies the unique times in which people like Giaus would have lived. The cult complex he would’ve worked in had a special purpose to create trust in the newly divided Empire into East and West and this specific archaeological piece shows how the cult wanted to show the relationship between Diocletian and Maximum. The Empire was undergoing a novel transformation, one where two Emperors ruled in supposed harmony. It was essential that Diocletian proved to the people that this new system in the Empire was efficacious, thus images like this were conjured.

This relief also offers us a grand understanding of how the imperial cult used religion as politics. The cult complex within Nicomedia was built at Diocletian's orders at the idea of it being a politically useful power. The complex not only held Temples to Gods and previous esteemed Emperors such as Julius Caesar, but it also presented itself in ways to exemplify the political stability of the present Empire. This relief shows this as it is a direct representation of the security and cohesion of the Empire, even with it being purposely split into two extensive regions to be led by two different Emperors.

## Faults in Historical Evidence

When looking to fully understand the extent in which the Imperial cult worked and used its power, we have many pieces of evidence to substantiate our conceptualizations. But, as with any time in history, there are indelible faults or gaps. One pertaining to Giaus and our development of his fictional character is that we don’t fully know how popular the imperial cult actually was and if it may have been all a performative practice. It is impossible for us to really know the balance between true public belief and support through imperial coercion. With this, it is difficult for us to even understand the full extent in which an imperial priest would live his day.

Being elites, cult priests had many reasons to be active and social. The Empire was an elitist society where wealth and popularity were paramount for success. Being close to the Emperor and politicians was also a major part of becoming highly esteemed. Because of this, we know many people used political things such as the cult to performatively show loyalty to the Emperor. This may mean that even people within the cult did not necessarily favor an Emperor or care about the rituals they did, but rather performed them out of hopes of gaining social attention.
