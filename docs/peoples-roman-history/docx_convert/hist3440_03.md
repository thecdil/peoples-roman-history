Aurelia Apollonia is a Roman citizen born and currently residing in Alexandria.

Her husband is well off enough to support her occupation as a midwife and allow

their family to be able to have a wet-nurse slave as well. Living in Alexandria they have the

influences of Kemetic and Roman religions intertwining. One of which are the cults

of Taweret and Bes which as a midwife and woman is a worship path that fits her lifestyle greatly.

However her Roman neighbors are wary. They worship the traditional Roman deities and they are fearful of the worship of two foreign gods that may seem abnormal. So when a birth in the community goes badly or ends in a death, public scrutiny turns on her and her worship.

In Roman society and many other ancient societies like it, religion is a major component of the

daily life of peoples living within the empire. One specialty about Roman Egypt where my character resides however is the integration of Kemetic deities into Roman religious practice.

Focus gods

Taweret

<img src="media/image2.png" style="width:1.77604in;height:3.10807in" />

Amulet of Taweret Roman Period 30 B.C.–A.D. 364

[<u>https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/575736</u>](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/575736)

Taweret is a composite goddess composed mostly in the form of a pregnant Hippopotamus with features of a crocodile and human. She is fierce like the main animal she represents and that ferocity helps her in the protection of mothers and children. She is a midwife goddess, protecting those who are in labor or who are post/pre child birth.

Religion:

Religion is an important part of humanity in any time period, past or present. Religion in Roman Egypt was no less important to individuals. Worship often took place in the home unless the individual was a priest of a certain god or royalty. Roman rule of Egypt led to a change of Egyptian temples and the rise of christianity eventually left temples destroyed or abandoned.

Maternal magic was popular for both Egypt and Roman societies. The death of mothers and the dangers of child birth were not uncommon. Before a time of germ theory and modern cleanliness, the solution to a safe birth was divinity.
