Aurelia Apollonia is a Roman citizen born and currently residing in Alexandria.

Her husband is well off enough to support her occupation as a midwife and allow their family to be able to have a wet-nurse slave as well. Living in Alexandria they have the influences of Kemetic and Roman religions intertwining. One of which are the cults of Taweret and Bes which as a midwife and woman is a worship path that fits her lifestyle greatly.

However her Roman neighbors are wary. They worship the traditional Roman deities and they are fearful of the worship of two foreign gods that may seem abnormal. So when a birth in the community goes badly or ends in a death, public scrutiny turns on her and her worship.

In Roman society and many other ancient societies like it, religion is a major component of the daily life of peoples living within the empire[^1]. One specialty about Roman Egypt where my character resides however is the integration of Kemetic deities into Roman religious practice[^2].

## Focus gods 

### Taweret

{% include essay/image.html objectid="Cannon03" caption="Amulet of Taweret Roman Period 30 B.C.–A.D. 364" %}

Taweret is a composite goddess composed mostly in the form of a pregnant Hippopotamus with features of a crocodile and human[^3]. She is fierce like the main animal she represents and that ferocity helps her in the protection of mothers and children[^4]. She is a midwife goddess, protecting those who are in labor or who are post/pre child birth. [^5]

Taweret did not have a proper temple or ritual center besides outside of the home. For families she was a goddess that lived and protected mothers and children away from religious centers and in their daily life. In a sense, the home became the temple. [^6]

## Bes

{% include essay/image.html objectid="Cannon05" caption="Bes Amulet Roman Period 30 B.C.–A.D. 364" %}

Bes, sometimes considered the cohort to Taweret, is a dwarf god of protection[^7]. Much like Taweret he is the protector of children and mothers[^8] and in mythology is the shield against danger for Horus the younger or Hopacrates[^9].

His protection involves looking fierce and is often portrayed with a deep scowl and intimidating look[^10].

## Religion

Religion is an important part of humanity in any time period, past or present. Religion in Roman Egypt was no less important to individuals. Worship often took place in the home unless the individual was a priest of a certain god or royalty[^11]. Roman rule of Egypt led to a change of Egyptian temples and the rise of christianity eventually left temples destroyed or abandoned[^12].

Maternal magic was popular for both Egypt and Roman societies. The death of mothers and the dangers of child birth were not uncommon. Before a time of germ theory and modern cleanliness, the solution to a safe birth was divinity[^13].

## Egypt vs. Rome

Egypt was under Roman rule after Alexander the Great’s invasion and take over of the land. The culture of these two lands despite being under one rule had their differences yet intertwined in many ways including religion[^14].

Roman Egyptian religion adopted composite gods of both Roman and Egyptian deities or just romanized Egyptian deities which created new iconography of each deity worshiped[^15].

## Religion in the life of Aurelia Apollonia

For Aurelia Apollonia, living in the province of Aegyptus as a married woman with a child must have held some influence in her life. The architecture and religious imagery no matter how ancient it was was a part of her everyday life and practice. She would have wanted the best for her child and herself and the goddesses who specialize directly in the protection of mothers would have been deeply intriguing.

In her home she would have had statues or jewelry with Taweret and Bes. A necklace with small beads in the form of the goddess may have been worn for protection over herself during pregnancy[^16]

Asking Bes to chase off illness and danger from her growing child and pray in relation to her growing baby would have been common.[^17]

In her work as a midwife, her practice most likely followed. Aurelia not only used worship in her own home but for others, caring for infants and mothers as a midwife.
